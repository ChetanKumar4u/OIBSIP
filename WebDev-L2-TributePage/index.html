01 THE EARLY YEARS

Curiosity was his first launchpad.

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born in 1931 in Rameswaram, a small island town in Tamil Nadu. His childhood was shaped by a close-knit family and the everyday life of a coastal community. He studied physics before moving to aerospace engineering, following an interest in flight that would guide his career.

After graduating from the Madras Institute of Technology, Kalam joined India's defence research organisation in 1958. He later moved to the Indian Space Research Organisation, where he led the team responsible for the SLV-III launch vehicle. In 1980, that work placed the Rohini satellite in orbit—an important step in India's independent space programme.

His public life expanded alongside his scientific work. Kalam returned to defence research, contributed to national technology programmes, and served as India's eleventh President from 2002 to 2007. He became known to many as the “People's President” for the warmth with which he met students and citizens.

After his presidency, he continued teaching, writing, and speaking with young people. In 2015, while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management Shillong, he died doing what had become central to his life: sharing ideas with students.
